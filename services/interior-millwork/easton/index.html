Interior millwork · Easton, PA

Interior millwork in Easton, PA

Easton's older neighborhoods each have a character of their own. Downtown, inside the city's historic district, is a dense grid of rowhomes and mixed-use buildings between the Delaware, the Lehigh and the Bushkill Creek. College Hill, above the Bushkill around Lafayette College, holds the city's largest homes, most built between 1830 and 1940. The West Ward, from Sixth Street out to 15th Street, is mostly Victorian homes built before 1940. Across the Lehigh, the South Side climbs a wooded hillside of twins, older singles and postwar capes. At Atlas Design & Build, we build and restore interior millwork, from built-ins and wainscoting to stair details, that suits the architecture of these homes. We live in Easton and manage every project directly.

Local knowledge

Millwork in Easton's older neighborhoods

The West Ward's Victorian homes were built with the kind of detail that is hard to find today: tall baseboards, deep window casings, turned stair balusters, pocket doors and built-in cabinetry. Decades of renovation and apartment conversions removed much of it. Restoring or recreating that trim is often the single change that makes these houses feel like themselves again.

College Hill holds the city's most elaborate millwork: paneled libraries, coffered ceilings, grand stairs and pocket doors in homes built between 1830 and 1940. Much of our work there is careful repair and matching, along with new built-ins designed to sit alongside original woodwork without competing with it.

Downtown rowhomes are narrow, and millwork there has to work hard. Built-ins that run the length of a wall, window seats in a front bay and storage under the stair add function without taking up floor space, and they can be profiled to match the original trim so they look like they were always there.

South Side homes span older twins and postwar capes. In the capes, built-ins under sloped ceilings, window seats in dormers and a well-proportioned trim package can give a simple house far more character. Across the city, we match existing profiles where they survive and design new ones that suit the age of the house where they do not.

Questions

Interior millwork in Easton: common questions

Can you match the original trim in our Easton home?

Usually, yes. We take profiles from surviving trim and have new pieces milled to match, so repairs and extensions blend with the original.

Can built-ins work in a narrow rowhome?

They are one of the best uses of space in a rowhome. Shallow built-ins along a long wall, window seats in a front bay and cabinetry under the stair add storage without narrowing the room.

Do you restore staircases?

Yes. We repair loose or missing balusters, rebuild handrails and restore newel posts, matching original profiles wherever they survive.
