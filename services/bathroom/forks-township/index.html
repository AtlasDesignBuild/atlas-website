Local context

Bathrooms in Forks Township homes

Most homes in Forks Township are newer construction or well maintained homes from recent decades, which means bathrooms here more often need layout modernization and finish upgrades than structural correction. Builder-grade bathrooms from the 1990s and 2000s are common, functional when the home was built, but overdue for the finishes, fixtures and layout that today's homeowners expect from a primary bath.

A smaller number of older farmhouses remain from the township's earlier history as farmland, and those bring the same older-home considerations we plan for elsewhere: older plumbing, knob and tube wiring and construction that requires assessment before design work begins. We are EPA Lead Safe certified for any home built before 1978. We assess site conditions on every project regardless of the home's age.

Our scope

What our bathroom remodeling services include in Forks Township

These are the bathroom projects we plan most often in Forks Township.

Removing corner whirlpool tubs

Primary bathrooms from the 1990s often have a large corner tub that rarely gets used. Replacing it with a bigger walk-in shower or a freestanding tub frees up floor space and simplifies cleaning.

Double vanities with real storage

Builder vanities offer little more than a sink base. Custom vanities with drawer banks, linen towers and a seated makeup area make a shared bathroom work for two people.

Large tiled showers with frameless glass

With the space a corner tub leaves behind, a shower can include a bench, niches and more than one shower head. Every shower is built over a continuous waterproofing membrane.

Kids' and hall bathrooms

Secondary bathrooms need durable tile, a tiled tub surround and storage that stands up to daily use. Many families remodel them alongside the primary bath to share trade visits.

Farmhouse bathrooms

Bathrooms in older farmhouses were often added later and squeezed into small rooms, sometimes with plumbing in exterior walls. We plan the layout and pipe routes so the new bathroom is comfortable and protected from freezing.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Should we keep a tub in the primary bathroom?

Many homeowners remove it if another bathroom in the house has one. If bathing matters to you, a freestanding tub takes less space than a built-in corner unit and becomes a focal point.

Can we remodel two bathrooms at the same time?

Yes, and it often saves time because the trades work both rooms in the same visits. We sequence the work so at least one shower in the house stays usable.

How large should a walk-in shower be?

Most primary bathrooms here can fit a shower five feet or more in length, which leaves room for a bench and a niche without crowding the rest of the room.
