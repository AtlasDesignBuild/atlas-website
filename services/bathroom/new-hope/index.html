Local knowledge

Remodeling bathrooms in New Hope and Solebury Township

New Hope Borough sits within a historic district, and many homes in New Hope and Solebury predate 1950, some by well over a century. Stone farmhouses, converted barns, carriage houses and 19th century homes along the canal all bring the same challenges to a bathroom: thick masonry walls, uneven floors, low ceilings and plumbing that has been rerouted many times.

Historic district review in the borough applies to exterior changes, such as a new window or an exterior vent, and we build that review into the schedule when a project calls for it. We are EPA Lead Safe certified for any home built before 1978, and we assess older bathrooms thoroughly at the first visit so conditions behind the walls are planned for rather than discovered mid-project.

Outside the borough, many Solebury homes rely on private wells and on-lot septic systems. Adding fixtures or a bathroom can affect a septic system, and depending on the change the Bucks County Department of Health may need to review it. Well water can also be hard or high in iron, which shapes our fixture and finish recommendations and whether water treatment belongs in the plan.

Questions

Bathroom remodeling in New Hope: common questions

Does the New Hope historic district affect a bathroom remodel?

Interior bathroom work is handled through the standard building permit. Historic district review in New Hope Borough applies to exterior changes, such as a new or resized window or an exterior vent. We plan for that review from the first conversation when a project needs it.

Can we add a bathroom to a home on a septic system?

Often yes, but it needs to be planned around the system's capacity. Depending on the change, the Bucks County Department of Health may need to review the septic system before new fixtures are added. We look at this early so it shapes the design rather than stalling it.

Will hard well water damage new fixtures and glass?

Hard or iron-rich water leaves scale and staining on glass, stone and fixtures over time. We recommend finishes and glass coatings that hold up well, and where water quality is a concern we suggest having it tested so treatment can be added as part of the project.
