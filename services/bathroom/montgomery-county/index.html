Our scope

What our bathroom remodeling services include in Montgomery County

Bathroom projects in Montgomery County tend to follow the age of the house. These are the ones we plan most often.

Original bathrooms in pre-war homes

Older bathrooms in Ambler and Blue Bell often have original tile, galvanized supply lines and little or no ventilation. We replace the plumbing while the walls are open and vent new fans to the outside.

Accessible showers for staying in your home

Curbless entries, wider doorways, built-in benches and blocking for grab bars make a bathroom easier to use for years to come. We plan these features so they look like design choices rather than equipment.

Primary suites in newer homes

Primary bathrooms in North Wales and Lansdale are often generous but finished with builder-grade materials. Tiled showers, frameless glass, double vanities and layered lighting transform them without moving walls.

Steam showers and soaking tubs

A steam shower needs a fully sealed enclosure, a sloped ceiling and a place for the generator. A soaking tub needs floor framing that can carry its weight when full. We plan both before the layout is final.

Heated floors and full waterproofing

Every shower is built over a continuous waterproofing membrane, and electric radiant mats can go under the floor tile on their own thermostat.

Permits across the county

Each township and borough reviews bathroom plumbing and electrical work on its own schedule. We file the permits and coordinate inspections so the work keeps moving.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can you build a curbless shower in an older home?

Often, yes. The shower floor has to sit lower than the surrounding floor, which we achieve by recessing the subfloor between the joists or building up the rest of the room. We check the framing before committing to the design.

What does a steam shower need?

A steam shower needs a fully sealed enclosure with a vapor barrier, a ceiling sloped so condensation runs off and a nearby space for the steam generator, often in a vanity or closet.

Do you replace old galvanized plumbing?

Yes. Galvanized supply lines corrode from the inside and reduce water pressure over time, so when the walls are open we replace them with new supply lines.
