Bathroom remodeling · Easton, PA

Bathroom remodeling in Easton, PA

Easton's older neighborhoods each have a character of their own. Downtown, inside the city's historic district, is a dense grid of rowhomes and mixed-use buildings between the Delaware, the Lehigh and the Bushkill Creek. College Hill, above the Bushkill around Lafayette College, holds the city's largest homes, most built between 1830 and 1940. The West Ward, from Sixth Street out to 15th Street, is mostly Victorian homes built before 1940. Across the Lehigh, the South Side climbs a wooded hillside of twins, older singles and postwar capes. At Atlas Design & Build, we design bathrooms that fit the way these houses were built, from tight rowhome baths to second floor rooms under Victorian rooflines. We live in Easton and manage every project directly.

Local knowledge

Bathrooms in Easton's older neighborhoods

In Easton's rowhomes, the bathroom is almost always upstairs, often at the back of the second floor above the kitchen, and it is usually the only full bath in the house. Plumbing runs in a tight chase, the floor has had a century to settle and the window may be the only source of ventilation. Adding a powder room on the first floor, or a second full bath upstairs, is one of the most valuable changes in these homes.

College Hill's larger homes often have more bathrooms, but many were added decades after the house was built, squeezed into closets, dressing rooms or the ends of hallways. Reworking those rooms into a proper primary bathroom, sometimes by borrowing space from an adjoining bedroom, is one of the most rewarding projects in the neighborhood.

The West Ward's Victorian houses, most built before 1940, bring details worth working with: tall ceilings, deep window casings and sometimes original tile. They also bring older supply lines, cast iron drains and plaster walls. Where a house was divided into apartments, it may have more bathrooms than the family needs, placed where the rental layout needed them rather than where they make sense today.

South Side homes range from older twins to postwar capes, where the upstairs bathroom sits under a sloped ceiling. Across the city, interior bathroom work goes through the building permit, and inside the downtown historic district a new or changed window visible from the street also needs a Certificate of Appropriateness.

Questions

Bathroom remodeling in Easton: common questions

Can we add a second bathroom to our Easton rowhome?

In many rowhomes, yes. Common locations include the space under the stairs, the back of the first floor near the kitchen or part of a large second floor bedroom. The key is routing the drain to the existing stack, which we map before designing.

Our house used to be apartments. Can you remove the extra bathroom?

Yes. Many West Ward homes were divided into units and have bathrooms in awkward places. We can remove or relocate them as part of returning the house to a single family layout, capping plumbing properly and rebuilding the space.

Do bathroom remodels in Easton need a permit?

Any bathroom remodel that changes plumbing, electrical or the layout needs a building permit from the City of Easton's Department of Planning and Codes. We pull the permit and coordinate inspections.
