Our scope

What our tile installation services include in Forks Township

These are the tile projects we install most often in Forks Township.

Large primary showers

Bigger showers allow benches, multiple niches and linear drains that let the floor tile run continuously. We lay out every cut line before installation begins.

Mudroom and laundry floors

Floors by the garage entry take the most wear in the house. Textured porcelain and darker grout hold up to boots, sports gear and pets while still looking finished.

Full-height kitchen backsplashes

Running tile to the ceiling behind a range or under open shelving gives a kitchen a more custom look. We center the layout on the range and windows so cuts land where they are not noticed.

Heated tile floors

Electric heating mats under bathroom or mudroom tile need a dedicated circuit and a programmable thermostat, which we plan with the electrical work.

Leveling farmhouse floors

Floors in older farmhouses are rarely level. We use leveling compound and an uncoupling membrane so new tile sits flat and stays crack free.

Local context

Tile in Forks Township homes

Most homes in Forks Township were built in the 1980s, 1990s and 2000s with builder-grade tile throughout, small ceramic floor tiles, basic tub surrounds, minimal grout quality. These materials have held up over time, but they show their age and rarely reflect how the homeowner actually wants the space to feel. Replacing tile in these homes is often straightforward: consistent framing, standard floor heights and no major substrate surprises in most cases.

A smaller number of older farmhouses on the township's remaining agricultural parcels may have older substrate conditions, including lath and plaster or uneven subfloors, that require more preparation before tile work begins. We are EPA Lead Safe certified for any home built before 1978. Regardless of the home's age, we assess substrate conditions before committing to an installation approach.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is heated flooring worth it?

It makes the most difference in bathrooms and mudrooms, especially over slabs or unheated spaces. The mats sit under the tile and are controlled by a thermostat, so they run only when you want them.

What tile works best in a mudroom?

Porcelain with some surface texture is the most durable choice. Pair it with a mid-tone or darker grout so daily wear does not show.

Should shower tile go all the way to the ceiling?

We usually recommend it. Full-height tile is easier to clean, protects the walls from moisture and makes the shower look taller.
