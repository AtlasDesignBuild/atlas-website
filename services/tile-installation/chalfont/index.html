Local knowledge

Tile work in Chalfont and New Britain Township

Chalfont's housing splits between the older homes of Chalfont Borough, incorporated in 1902 around its railroad station, and the colonials and split levels built across New Britain Township in the second half of the 20th century. In the borough, tile projects often involve settled floors and plaster walls. In the township, they usually start with a dated primary shower or a kitchen floor.

The borough sits between the north and main branches of the Neshaminy Creek, and many homes in the area have finished or partly finished basements. Tile is a smart finish below grade because it tolerates moisture, but only when the slab has been tested and the right membrane and mortar are used.

Mudrooms and laundry rooms are also common projects here, especially in township colonials where the side or garage entry takes most of the family's daily traffic.

Questions

Tile installation in Chalfont: common questions

Is tile a good choice for a basement floor in Chalfont?

Yes, when it is installed correctly. Porcelain does not absorb water and holds up well below grade. We test the slab for moisture first and choose a membrane and mortar suited to the conditions.

What tile holds up best in a mudroom?

Through-body porcelain with a textured or matte finish is the most durable choice. It resists scratches, hides dirt and stays safer underfoot when wet. A darker grout also helps the floor stay looking clean.

Do you take on tile projects that are not part of a larger remodel?

Yes. We work as a tile contractor on standalone projects, from a single shower surround to a full floor, anywhere in Chalfont Borough or New Britain Township.
