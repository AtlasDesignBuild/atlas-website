Local knowledge

Tile work in Langhorne and Middletown Township

Many homes with a Langhorne mailing address date from Middletown Township's building boom in the 1950s. Bathrooms from that era were often tiled the old way, with wall tile set in a thick mortar bed over metal lath. That tile is remarkably durable, which is why so much of it survives in pink, green, blue and yellow, but removing it is heavy work that takes the wall down to the studs.

Many of these homes also sit on concrete slabs, some with radiant heating in the slab. Tile is an excellent finish over a heated slab, but slab cracks will telegraph through new tile unless a crack isolation membrane goes down first. We map existing cracks and plan the membrane accordingly.

In Langhorne Borough's older homes, the challenges shift to settled wood floors and plaster walls. We level and stiffen the floor where needed so new tile stays sound for the long term.

Questions

Tile installation in Langhorne: common questions

Is it worth replacing the original 1950s tile in our bathroom?

If the tile is intact and you like it, it can be kept and refreshed with new grout and fixtures. If you want a different look, removal is the right call, but plan for the wall to come down to the studs, since that era's tile is usually set in a mortar bed over metal lath.

Can you tile over a concrete slab with cracks?

Yes, with the right preparation. We install a crack isolation membrane that lets the slab move slightly without cracking the tile. Larger or uneven cracks are evaluated and repaired first.

Can we tile over a floor with radiant heat in the slab?

Yes. Tile is one of the best finishes over radiant heat because it conducts warmth well. We take care not to damage the heating lines during removal and preparation.
