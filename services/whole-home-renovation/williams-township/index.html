Local context

Whole home renovations in Williams Township

Farmhouses here often combine a stone or timber original with later frame additions, each built to the standards of its time. Floors change level from room to room, and systems have been patched together over decades. Newer custom homes on larger lots tend to need a different kind of whole home project: updating finishes and reworking rooms so the house suits how the family lives now.

Our scope

What our whole home renovation services include in Williams Township

Whole home projects in Williams Township usually include several of these.

Joining original house and additions

We level floors where we can, align ceilings and rework transitions so rooms from different eras feel like one house.

Replacing patched-together systems

Electrical, plumbing and heating in older farmhouses are often a mix of eras. A whole home project is the time to replace them properly.

Preserving what makes the house worth keeping

Stone walls, wide plank floors, beams and deep window sills are restored and worked into the new design.

Updating custom homes from the 1980s and 1990s

In newer homes, we replace dated finishes, rework underused formal rooms and carry one set of materials through the whole house.

Our approach

Why Williams Township homeowners choose Atlas

A farmhouse renovation is part restoration and part new construction. We decide with you what to preserve, what to rebuild and what to leave alone, then manage both kinds of work under one plan.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How do you decide what to keep in an old farmhouse?

We walk the house with you and sort features into three groups: worth restoring, worth reusing elsewhere and better replaced. That plan guides the design and the budget.

Can we live in a farmhouse during a whole home renovation?

Sometimes, if the work can be phased by section of the house. Projects that replace electrical or heating systems throughout may need a short period away, which we plan before construction starts.
