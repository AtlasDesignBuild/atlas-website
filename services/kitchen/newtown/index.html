Local knowledge

Remodeling kitchens in Newtown Borough and Newtown Township

Newtown Borough, founded in 1684, has some of the oldest homes in Bucks County, with Federal, Greek Revival and Victorian houses along State Street and the surrounding blocks. Kitchens in these homes are often narrow rooms at the back of the house, sometimes in additions from different decades, with uneven floors and walls that are rarely square. Cabinetry built to fit those conditions matters more here than anywhere else.

Newtown Township grew around the borough through planned communities such as Newtown Grant, built by Orleans in fourteen sections. Township kitchens from the 1980s and 1990s usually have the right general layout but carry the marks of their era: soffits above the upper cabinets, oak or cherry cabinetry, a peninsula where an island would work better and lighting limited to a single ceiling fixture.

Removing soffits is one of the most common requests in township homes, and it is worth planning carefully because soffits often hide ductwork, plumbing or wiring that needs a new route. In communities with a homeowners association, exterior changes such as a new hood vent through an outside wall can also need association approval, so we review those rules early.

Questions

Kitchen remodeling in Newtown: common questions

Can the soffits in our Newtown kitchen be removed?

Usually, yes. Soffits often conceal ductwork, a vent stack or wiring, so removing them means finding a new route for whatever is inside. We open a small inspection area or review the framing early so the design and the budget account for it.

Do we need HOA approval for a kitchen remodel in Newtown?

Interior work usually does not, but exterior changes such as a new range hood vent, a window change or a new door often do. Many associations also set rules for work hours, dumpsters and contractor parking. We review your association's requirements before scheduling.

Is my home in Newtown Borough or Newtown Township?

They share a name and mailing address but are separate municipalities with separate permit offices. The borough is the historic core around State Street and the township surrounds it. We confirm which applies to your property before filing.
