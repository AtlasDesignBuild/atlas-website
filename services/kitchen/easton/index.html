Kitchen remodeling · Easton, PA

Kitchen remodeling in Easton, PA

Easton's older neighborhoods each have a character of their own. Downtown, inside the city's historic district, is a dense grid of rowhomes and mixed-use buildings between the Delaware, the Lehigh and the Bushkill Creek. College Hill, above the Bushkill around Lafayette College, holds the city's largest homes, most built between 1830 and 1940. The West Ward, from Sixth Street out to 15th Street, is mostly Victorian homes built before 1940. Across the Lehigh, the South Side climbs a wooded hillside of twins, older singles and postwar capes. At Atlas Design & Build, we plan kitchens around the realities of these homes, from narrow rowhome footprints to kitchens tucked into rear additions. We live in Easton and manage every project directly.

Local knowledge

Kitchens in Easton's older neighborhoods

Downtown and West Ward rowhomes are narrow and deep, and the kitchen is usually at the back of the house, often in a rear addition with its own floor level and ceiling height. The layout is almost always a galley or an L, and the most useful projects rethink that long room as a whole: moving the sink under the window, carrying cabinetry to the ceiling and opening the kitchen toward the dining room where the structure allows.

College Hill's larger Victorian, Federal and Revival homes have more room to work with, but their kitchens were often designed as service spaces, closed off from the main rooms with a butler's pantry or back stair between. Recovering those spaces and opening the kitchen to a family room or breakfast room is the most common College Hill project we plan. College Hill is listed on the National Register of Historic Places but sits outside the city's local historic district, so work there goes through the standard building permit.

Many West Ward homes were divided into apartments over the years, and some still carry a second kitchen upstairs. When a house is being returned to a single family home, removing the extra kitchen and rebuilding the main one is often the centerpiece of the project. Victorian houses of this age are frequently balloon framed, so when walls are opened we add fire blocking as part of the work.

South Side homes sit on a hillside, and many have a lower level at grade on one side, which can open options for a kitchen with a walk-out. Inside the downtown historic district, exterior changes visible from the street, such as a new window or rear door, need a Certificate of Appropriateness, while interior kitchen work anywhere in the city goes through the building permit.

Questions

Kitchen remodeling in Easton: common questions

Can you open up a rowhome kitchen in Easton?

Often, yes. In many Downtown and West Ward rowhomes, the wall between the kitchen and dining room can be opened or widened with a properly sized beam. Party walls shared with neighbors stay in place, so the plan works with the width of the house rather than against it.

Do we need historic approval for a kitchen remodel downtown?

Interior work does not need a Certificate of Appropriateness. Inside the downtown historic district, exterior changes visible from a street or public way, such as a new window, door or hood vent, do. We plan for that review early when a project calls for it.

Who issues kitchen remodeling permits in Easton?

The City of Easton's Department of Planning and Codes issues building permits within the city. We prepare the application, pull the permit and coordinate inspections.
