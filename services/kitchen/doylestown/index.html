Older construction

Remodeling older homes in Doylestown

A significant share of homes in Doylestown Borough predate modern wiring and plumbing standards. Knob and tube electrical, galvanized plumbing, lath and plaster walls and settling foundations are common in homes built before 1950. These are not obstacles. They are part of what makes older Doylestown homes worth renovating carefully. We are EPA Lead Safe certified, which is required for any home built before 1978. We plan for the realities of older construction rather than being surprised by them mid-project.

Doylestown Borough also sits within a historic overlay district. For kitchen remodels this rarely affects the interior work, but if your project involves windows, exterior openings or additions, there are additional review steps we factor into the timeline from the start.

Local knowledge

Remodeling kitchens in Doylestown Borough and Doylestown Township

Kitchens in Doylestown Borough's Victorian and early 20th century homes were rarely designed as the center of the house. They tend to sit at the rear, sometimes beside a back stair or pantry, and many were expanded or relocated in later additions. The best projects here recover that surrounding space, such as a butler's pantry, a back hall or an enclosed porch, and give the kitchen a layout that finally suits the house.

Doylestown Township, which surrounds the borough and is a separate municipality, is largely postwar, with ranches, split levels and colonials alongside larger homes from the 1990s and 2000s. Kitchens there are structurally simpler and often ready for a bigger change, such as removing a wall to the family room or rebuilding around a new island.

The borough's Historical and Architectural Review Board reviews exterior changes visible from a public street within the historic district. Interior kitchen work is exempt, although it still needs a building permit. A new window, a relocated door or an exterior range hood vent on a street-facing wall would need review, so we plan the venting route with that in mind.

Questions

Kitchen remodeling in Doylestown: common questions

Does the Doylestown HARB review kitchen remodels?

Interior work is exempt from review by the Historical and Architectural Review Board, but it still needs a building permit. Exterior changes visible from a public street within the historic district, such as a new window, door or hood vent, do require review.

Where does the range hood vent go in an older borough home?

Ideally through a rear or side wall that is not visible from the street, or up through the roof. We plan the duct route early, since it affects cabinet layout, the hood size and whether historic review is needed.

Is my home in Doylestown Borough or Doylestown Township?

The borough is the historic center of town and the township surrounds it. They share a mailing address but are separate municipalities with different permit offices. We confirm the correct one before any work is scheduled.
