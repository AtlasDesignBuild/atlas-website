Local context

Kitchens in Forks Township homes

Most homes in Forks Township are newer construction or well maintained homes from recent decades, which means kitchens here more often need layout modernization and finish upgrades than structural correction. The housing stock is generally in good condition, but kitchens that made sense when the home was built in the 1980s, 1990s or 2000s often no longer match how families use the space today.

A smaller number of older farmhouses remain from the township's earlier history as farmland, and those bring the same older-home considerations we plan for elsewhere: older wiring, plumbing and structural quirks that require assessment before design work begins. We are EPA Lead Safe certified for any home built before 1978. We assess site conditions on every project regardless of the home's age.

Our scope

What our kitchen remodeling services include in Forks Township

Forks Township kitchens tend to fall into a few types. Here is how we approach each one.

Islands sized for open family rooms

Kitchens that open to a family room often have an island that is too small or a peninsula that interrupts traffic. We resize or replace it so seating, prep space and walkways all work at the same time.

Replacing first-generation cabinetry

Cabinets from the 1980s and 1990s often stop short of the ceiling and waste corner space. Custom cabinetry built to the ceiling, with organized interiors, adds real storage without changing the footprint.

Pantries and drop zones

Larger Forks kitchens usually have room for a walk-in or cabinet pantry, a coffee station or a drop zone near the garage entry. We plan these with the main layout rather than adding them as an afterthought.

Farmhouse kitchens with character

In older farmhouses, the kitchen often sits in a later rear addition with uneven floors and walls. We level and reframe where needed and design cabinetry that suits the age of the house.

Ventilation for professional ranges

Larger ranges and hoods need properly sized duct runs and, above certain fan capacities, make-up air. We plan ventilation, gas and electrical before the final appliance list is set.

Hoods and inserts made in house

Our range hoods and glass cabinet inserts are fabricated in house, which lets us size the hood to the range below it and match the metal finish to your hardware.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can you add an island to a kitchen that doesn't have one?

Often, yes. An island needs roughly 42 inches of clearance on its working sides, so we check the room dimensions first, along with the flooring under the existing cabinets and whether plumbing or power needs to run to the island.

Do we need new flooring when we replace the cabinets?

Usually. New layouts rarely match the old cabinet footprint exactly, which leaves gaps in the existing floor. If the same flooring runs into the family room, we plan the replacement across both spaces.

Is a kitchen remodel worth it in a newer home?
