Farmhouse kitchen ideas for Bucks County homes

Farmhouse kitchens fit older Bucks County homes because the stone and fieldstone farmhouses here already carry the character the style echoes. The elements that work best are shaker cabinetry, an apron-front sink placed at a window and open shelving used in a short run rather than across the whole kitchen.

By Rachel Foy, Co-founder & Design · Kitchen remodeling · Published October 5, 2026

Reviewed by John J. Foy, Co-founder & Design

Farmhouse style suits Bucks County in a way it does not suit every market. The stone and fieldstone farmhouses throughout the county already carry the architectural character farmhouse kitchens are designed to echo, which means the style reads as authentic here rather than applied.

This guide walks through the farmhouse elements that work best in these homes and how to keep the style from tipping into something more themed than designed. If you are planning a kitchen remodel in an older Bucks County home, these are the decisions we work through with clients most often.

Why farmhouse style fits Bucks County's older homes

A farmhouse kitchen works best in a home with the architecture to support it, deep window sills, exposed beams, stone or fieldstone construction, the kind of detail common throughout older Bucks County homes. In a newer home without that architectural backdrop, the same finishes can read as a style applied for its own sake rather than a kitchen that belongs to the house around it.

Shaker cabinetry, the foundation of a farmhouse kitchen

A door that works in either direction. Shaker style cabinetry, with its simple recessed panel, is the most common starting point for a farmhouse kitchen, and for good reason, it suits both a traditional and a more modern farmhouse direction without needing to be replaced if the rest of the kitchen's style shifts over time.

Painted finishes that suit the style. Painted finishes in off white, sage or soft black all read well within the style.

Apron-front sinks and where they make sense

Placed where it can be seen. An apron-front, or farmhouse, sink is one of the most recognizable elements of the style, and it works best at a window with a clear sightline, since the deep basin and exposed front are meant to be seen, not tucked beneath a run of cabinetry.

Settle the cabinet base early. Choosing the right cabinet base to support the sink's weight and depth is a detail worth settling early in design rather than after cabinetry is ordered.

Open shelving, used with intention

A run, not a wall. Open shelving is a farmhouse staple, but it works best used selectively, a run of two or three shelves rather than the full upper cabinet run, holding items that are actually used daily rather than purely decorative pieces.

Mind the maintenance. Used throughout an entire kitchen, open shelving usually creates more maintenance than most households want day to day.

Modern farmhouse versus traditional farmhouse

Traditional farmhouse leans into more ornamentation, beadboard panels, visible hardware, a more layered, collected look.

Modern farmhouse pares the same elements back, flat panel or shaker cabinetry, simpler hardware, a more restrained material palette, while keeping the apron sink and natural wood tones that anchor the style.

Most of the kitchens we design in Bucks County land closer to modern farmhouse, since it ages better and suits a wider range of adjacent rooms.

Hardware Visible, often black iron or bin pulls Simple bar or knob, matte black or brass

Frequently asked questions

Does farmhouse style work in a smaller kitchen?

Yes, though scale matters more in a smaller space. A single apron sink and shaker cabinetry carry the style without needing open shelving or heavier fixtures that can crowd a small room. Our small kitchen ideas guide covers this in more depth.

Is farmhouse style still relevant, or is it dated?

Farmhouse has shifted meaningfully toward the more restrained modern farmhouse direction in recent years, which has kept it from feeling dated the way a more ornamental, trend-driven kitchen style can. Choosing the modern farmhouse version of each element helps a kitchen hold up over time.

What counters pair best with a farmhouse kitchen?

Natural stone, soapstone and butcher block all suit the style well. A quartz counter in a softer, less veined pattern also works, particularly in a modern farmhouse direction.

Can I mix farmhouse elements with a more contemporary kitchen?

Yes, this is common and often reads as more intentional than a fully themed farmhouse kitchen. An apron sink or a single run of open shelving can bring in the character without committing the whole room to the style.

How much does an apron-front sink add to a kitchen remodel budget?

It typically costs more than a standard undermount sink, both for the fixture itself and for the cabinet modification needed to support it properly. Our kitchen remodel cost guide covers overall project ranges this fits within.

Does farmhouse style work with a kitchen backsplash?

Yes, a simple subway tile or a natural stone backsplash both suit the style well. Our kitchen backsplash tile ideas guide covers backsplash choices that work across kitchen styles, farmhouse included.
