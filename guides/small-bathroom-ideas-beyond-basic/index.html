Bathroom remodeling

Small bathroom ideas beyond a basic remodel

By Rachel Foy · Bathroom remodeling · Published September 8, 2026

A small bathroom does not have to look or feel small, and it does not have to be a purely functional room either. Once the layout fundamentals are settled, tile placement, fixture positions, storage, there is a second set of decisions that separates a basic small bathroom remodel from one that feels genuinely considered. This guide covers those upgrades.

If you have not yet worked through the fundamentals, our guide to small bathroom remodel ideas that actually work covers layout and material basics first. This guide picks up from there.

Why small does not mean basic

A small bathroom is often the room homeowners are most tempted to under-invest in, on the logic that a smaller room needs less. In practice, a small bathroom is seen up close, at eye level, every single day, which means the details matter more here than in almost any other room in the house. A handful of considered upgrades change a small bathroom from purely functional to genuinely finished.

Floating vanities and visual space

A wall mounted, floating vanity opens up the floor beneath it, which makes a small bathroom read as more spacious than the square footage suggests. It also simplifies cleaning, since there is no base cabinet trapping moisture against the floor. This is one of the highest impact, moderate cost upgrades we install in small bathrooms.

Curbless showers in a small footprint

A curbless, zero threshold shower entry removes the visual break a traditional curb creates, letting the floor read as one continuous surface from the vanity through the shower. It requires more planning around slope and waterproofing than a standard curb, which is why it is worth discussing early in design rather than adding late. In a small bathroom, this one change does more for the sense of space than almost any material choice.

Statement lighting as the focal point

In a small bathroom, a single well chosen light fixture, a sculptural sconce, an unexpected pendant instead of a standard vanity light, can do the work an entire accent wall does in a larger room. Because the fixture only has to fill a small space, it is also one of the more affordable ways to make a small bathroom feel intentional rather than purely functional.

Pattern and wallpaper, low risk in a small room

A patterned or wallpapered ceiling, or a fully wallpapered powder room, is a lower risk way to bring in a bold choice than it would be in a larger room, since the material cost and installation scope stay contained. Moisture rated wallpaper options have improved significantly, which makes this a realistic option even in a full bathroom with a shower, not just a powder room.

Heated floors, worth it in a small space

Heated floors are one of the more requested upgrades in small bathrooms specifically, since the smaller footprint keeps installation cost more contained than it would be in a larger primary bathroom. It is also one of the upgrades clients tell us they notice daily, more than almost any finish choice. For homeowners in Doylestown and neighboring communities where winters are cold, this is consistently among the most appreciated details in a finished bathroom.

Frequently asked questions

Are floating vanities harder to maintain?

No, they are generally easier to keep clean since there is no base cabinet trapping moisture against the floor. They do require solid blocking behind the wall for a secure mount, which we plan for during rough-in.

Is a curbless shower more expensive than a standard shower?

It typically adds cost due to the additional waterproofing and slope planning required, though the difference is more modest than most homeowners expect, particularly when it is planned from the start rather than added as a change partway through.

Is wallpaper practical in a bathroom with a shower?

Modern moisture rated wallpaper has made this realistic in a full bathroom, not just a powder room, though we still recommend it away from direct water contact, on the ceiling or on walls outside the wet zone.

Are these upgrades worth it in a bathroom I might sell in a few years?

Most of these upgrades read well to buyers and are not overly personal choices, particularly floating vanities and heated floors. Wallpaper and bold lighting are lower cost to change later if a future buyer's taste differs.

Should I do all of these upgrades, or pick one or two?

Most small bathrooms benefit most from choosing one or two upgrades to invest in fully rather than spreading a limited budget thin across all of them. We help clients prioritize based on daily use and the overall project budget.
