Kitchen remodel cost calculator

Cabinetry typically takes 30% to 35% of a kitchen remodel budget, labor 20% to 25%, appliances 12% to 18% and counters 10% to 15%. Those proportions hold whether the total budget is $45,000 or $150,000.

Most kitchen remodel budgets break down the same way, even when the total varies widely. Cabinetry, counters, appliances and labor consistently make up the largest shares, in roughly the same proportions, whether the total budget is $45,000 or $150,000.

This guide walks through that breakdown so you can estimate how your own budget splits across categories once you have a target total in mind. How a kitchen remodel budget typically breaks down

Across the kitchens we build, the same categories consistently account for most of the budget, cabinetry, counters, appliances, labor, and a smaller remainder split across flooring, backsplash, lighting, plumbing fixtures and permits. The exact percentages shift somewhat by project, a kitchen with custom cabinetry skews higher on that line, a kitchen keeping existing appliances skews lower there, but the framework holds across most remodels.

Working an example at a $60,000 budget

Applying the ranges above to a $60,000 remodel gives a working estimate:

  • Cabinetry. Roughly $19,500
  • Counters. Roughly $7,500
  • Appliances. Roughly $9,000
  • Labor. Roughly $13,500
  • Flooring. Roughly $4,200
  • Backsplash and fixtures. Roughly $4,200
  • Permits and design fees. Roughly $2,100

These are starting points, not fixed allocations, a homeowner prioritizing custom cabinetry over a larger appliance package would shift the split accordingly.

Where a budget breakdown shifts by project type

  • Cosmetic refresh. A cosmetic refresh that keeps the existing layout shifts more of the budget toward counters, cabinet fronts and finishes, with little or nothing allocated to structural labor.
  • Full remodel with a layout change. A full remodel with a layout change shifts more toward labor and often toward cabinetry, since custom or semi-custom cabinetry becomes more central to solving a new layout.

Frequently asked questions

Is this breakdown accurate for every kitchen remodel?

It reflects typical proportions across the kitchens we build, but every project is different. A kitchen prioritizing a particular category, custom cabinetry or a high-end appliance package, shifts the proportions accordingly.

Should I set my total budget first or build it up from each category?

Both approaches work, and we generally recommend starting with a total range in mind, then using a breakdown like this one to sanity check whether that total supports the priorities that matter most to you.

What is usually the easiest place to cut cost if my budget is tight?

Cabinetry finish level and appliance package tend to offer the most flexibility without compromising the kitchen's function, semi-custom cabinetry instead of fully custom, or a mid-range appliance package instead of a premium one.

Does this breakdown include demolition and site prep?

Demolition and site prep are generally included within the labor category above, though a project with significant structural changes may see labor's share run higher than the typical range.

How accurate is a percentage-based estimate compared to a formal quote?

It is a useful planning tool for an early budget conversation, not a substitute for a project-specific quote, which accounts for your home's actual layout, condition and material selections.

Can I use this same breakdown for a bathroom or basement remodel?

The categories shift for other room types, a bathroom budget skews more toward plumbing fixtures and tile, for example. Reach out and we can walk through a breakdown specific to your project type.
