How much do custom built-ins cost in Bucks County?

By Atlas Design & Build · Interior millwork · Published August 15, 2026

Custom built-ins are one of the more requested additions on remodeling projects across Bucks County — whether that is a media wall in a basement, a window seat with storage in a mudroom, or a full wall of bookshelves flanking a fireplace. They also carry one of the widest cost ranges of anything we build, since the difference between a simple shelving unit and a fully custom, furniture grade piece is significant.

This guide breaks down what custom built-ins typically cost through our interior millwork service and what drives the number from one end of the range to the other.

What pushes cost toward the high end

  • Furniture grade hardwood rather than painted MDF or poplar
  • Fully custom sizing to fit an irregular wall, alcove or ceiling height
  • Integrated lighting within the millwork
  • Glass front cabinet doors or custom metal and glass details, an Atlas specialty
  • Matching existing trim profiles in an older home exactly
  • Built-in seating with upholstery

What keeps cost in the lower range

  • Standard, modular dimensions rather than a fully custom fit
  • Painted finish on MDF or poplar rather than stained hardwood
  • Open shelving rather than a mix of open and closed storage with doors
  • Simpler, cleaner profiles rather than applied moulding detail

Where built-ins tend to go in a home

Living rooms and family rooms are the most common location, typically flanking a fireplace or built along a full wall. Basements are close behind, where built-ins often double as both storage and the visual anchor of a finished space. Mudrooms and entryways are another frequent request, where built-in lockers or a bench with storage solve a real organization problem that a homeowner has usually been living with for years. Home offices and primary bedroom closets round out the most common locations.

Built-ins versus stock furniture

A built-in costs more than a comparable piece of freestanding furniture, but it does something furniture cannot — it uses the full space, including awkward corners, irregular ceiling heights and odd wall widths that stock furniture is never sized for. In an older Bucks County home, where rooms are rarely perfectly square or a standard width, this matters more than it does in newer construction.

A note on timelines

Custom millwork has its own lead time separate from the rest of a remodel — often four to eight weeks for fabrication once the design is finalized, in addition to installation time on site. If built-ins are part of a larger remodel, we plan this lead time in alongside cabinetry and other custom elements so it does not become the item that delays a project close to the finish line.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to buy furniture instead of building custom?

Often yes, on a pure dollar basis. The value of a built-in is in the fit, the use of otherwise wasted space, and the finished, permanent look — not in being a lower cost alternative to furniture.

What material is most common for built-ins?

Painted poplar or MDF is the most common and most cost effective choice for a painted finish. For a stained, wood grain look, we most often use oak, maple or cherry depending on the room and the client's preference.

Can built-ins include lighting?

Yes, integrated lighting — typically LED strip lighting inside shelving or cabinetry — is a common upgrade and one of the details that makes a built-in feel more finished and more custom.

Do built-ins add value to a home?

They are generally viewed positively by buyers, particularly functional built-ins like mudroom storage, though they are more of a livability investment than a pure resale play. Our kitchen remodel ROI guide covers this distinction in more depth for kitchen specific work.

How long does a built-in project take from start to finish?

Design and fabrication typically take four to eight weeks, with installation ranging from a few days to about a week depending on scope.

Can built-ins be added to an existing room without a full remodel?

Yes, built-ins are one of the more common standalone projects we take on outside of a larger remodel, since they do not require the same scope of demolition or disruption.
